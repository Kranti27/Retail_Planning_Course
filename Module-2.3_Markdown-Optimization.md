## What Markdowns Are (First Principles)

### Why Markdowns Exist

In a perfect world, every product sells at full price. In reality, 25–40% of lifestyle retail inventory will eventually be discounted. Why?

| Root Cause | What Went Wrong | Markdown Type |
|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| Over-buying | Bought more than demand warranted | Clearance |
| Wrong product selection | Customer didn't want this design | Clearance |
| Wrong allocation | Right product, wrong stores | Can be fixed by reallocation BEFORE markdown |
| Late delivery | Product arrived after peak selling window | Seasonal clearance |
| Price too high | Customer perceived product as overpriced | Permanent price adjustment |
| Competitive pressure | Competitor selling similar at lower price | Competitive response |
| End of season | Natural lifecycle — fashion products become stale | Planned EOSS |
| Damaged/shopworn | Product quality deteriorated on floor | Distress sale |

**The critical distinction:** Some markdowns are **unavoidable** (end-of-season clearance on fashion products). Others are **avoidable** (clearing products that were over-bought or wrongly allocated). The planner's job is to minimize the avoidable ones.

### The True Cost of Markdowns (Beyond the Discount)

A 30% markdown on a $30 product seems to "cost" $9 per unit. But the actual cost is higher:

| Cost Component | Description | Estimated Value |
|---------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Direct margin loss | $9 per unit × units marked down | Visible |
| Customer conditioning | Customers learn to wait for sales | Revenue leak in future seasons |
| Brand perception damage | "Always on sale" = "not worth full price" | Long-term brand erosion |
| Operational cost | Re-tagging, re-merchandising, markdown admin | $5-10 per unit |
| Opportunity cost | Capital tied in markdown stock could have been invested in fast sellers | Invisible but real |
| Competitive signal | Competitors see your markdowns and adjust their strategies | Strategic risk |

**Rule of thumb:** The true cost of a markdown is 1.5–2× the visible discount amount.

## The Markdown Decision Framework

### Step 1: Diagnose — Why Isn't It Selling?

Before marking down, diagnose the root cause. Different causes demand different solutions:

| Symptom | Possible Cause | Right Action | Wrong Action |
|---------|---------------|-------------|-------------|
| Low sell-through in ALL stores | Wrong product or wrong price | Markdown or RTV | Reallocate (won't help) |
| Low sell-through in SOME stores | Wrong stores, right product | Reallocate to better stores | Markdown (premature) |
| Decent sell-through but stock too high | Over-bought | Markdown on excess | Wait (stock will age) |
| Slow start, picking up week by week | Product is in Introduction stage | Wait, give it time | Markdown (too early) |
| Strong start, now declining | Product entering Decline | Markdown now before it gets worse | Reorder (throwing good money after bad) |

**The biggest markdown mistake:** Marking down a product that was allocated to the wrong stores. Reallocation costs $2-3 per unit (logistics). A markdown costs $9-15 per unit (margin loss). Always check if reallocation can solve the problem BEFORE marking down.

### Step 2: Decide — Markdown vs. Alternatives

| Alternative | When It Works | Limitation |
|------------|--------------|-----------|
| **Reallocation** (transfer to faster stores) | Product sells well in SOME stores but not others | Logistics cost, time delay |
| **Bundle** (pair with a fast seller) | Product is decent but needs a push | Dilutes margin on the fast seller |
| **Return to Vendor (RTV)** | Vendor agreement allows returns | Not all vendors accept RTV |
| **Hold for next season** | Product is "carry-over-able" (not fashion-dated) | Holding cost, aging risk, working capital lock |
| **Outlet/channel switch** | Outlet stores or online clearance channel exists | Not all retailers have outlets |
| **Markdown** | None of the above work, or stock age demands action | Direct margin hit |

### Step 3: Time It — When to Pull the Trigger

**The markdown timing curve:**

```
MARGIN RETAINED (%)
    100% ├──────────────────────────────────────────
         │ ■■■■■■■■■■
     90% │           ■■■■■
         │                ■■■■
     80% │                    ■■■■
         │                        ■■■
     70% │                           ■■■■      ← Sweet spot: markdown HERE
         │                               ■■■■     (20-30% off, Week 10-12)
     60% │                                   ■■■■
         │                                       ■■■
     50% │                                          ■■■■  ← Too late: forced
         │                                              ■■   to go 40-50% off
     40% ├──────────────────────────────────────────────■■■
         W1  W2  W4  W6  W8  W10  W12  W14  W16  W18  W20
                        SEASON WEEK
```

**The timing paradox:** Early markdowns are less deep (you can clear at 20% off) but you sacrifice full-price selling time. Late markdowns require deeper discounts (40-50% off) because the remaining window is short.

**The optimal markdown window depends on:**

**Markdown Trigger Week = Total Season Weeks × (1 - Target Sell-Through at Full Price)**

If season = 16 weeks and you target 65% sell-through at full price:

**Markdown Trigger Week = 16 × (1 - 0.65) = 16 × 0.35 = Week 5.6 from end = Week 10.4**

So around Week 10-11, you should evaluate: is the product on track for 65% sell-through? If not, activate markdown.

## Markdown Depth Strategy: How Much Off?

### The Graduated Markdown Approach

Don't go straight to 50% off. Use a graduated approach:

| Phase | Typical Discount | Duration | Expected Response |
|-------|-----------------|----------|-------------------|
| Phase 1 | 15-20% off | 2-3 weeks | Moderate — clears price-sensitive fence-sitters |
| Phase 2 | 25-30% off | 2-3 weeks | Strong — attracts deal seekers |
| Phase 3 | 40-50% off | 2-4 weeks | Very strong — final clearance |
| Phase 4 (if needed) | 60-70% off or write-off | Terminal | Last resort — salvage what you can |

### Calculating Markdown Depth by Product Type

The right markdown depth depends on the product's price elasticity (Module 2.2):

**Worked Example — Two Products:**

**Product A: Decorative Cushion Cover (fashion, elastic)**

| Discount | Price | Est. Units/Week | Weekly Revenue | Weekly Margin (Cost $11.40) |
|----------|-------|-----------------|---------------|----------------------------|
| 0% | $30.00 | 800 | $24,000 | $14,880 |
| 20% | $24.00 | 1,400 | $33,600 | $17,640 |
| 30% | $21.00 | 2,000 | $42,000 | $19,200 |
| 40% | $18.00 | 2,400 | $43,200 | $15,840 |
| 50% | $15.00 | 2,600 | $39,000 | $9,360 |

**Optimal markdown depth: 30%.** Revenue peaks at 40% off, but MARGIN peaks at 30% off. Beyond 30%, each additional unit sold generates diminishing margin.

**Product B: Basic Cotton Throw (staple, less elastic)**

| Discount | Price | Est. Units/Week | Weekly Revenue | Weekly Margin (Cost $17.00) |
|----------|-------|-----------------|---------------|----------------------------|
| 0% | $45.00 | 500 | $22,500 | $14,000 |
| 20% | $36.00 | 700 | $25,200 | $13,300 |
| 30% | $31.50 | 800 | $25,200 | $11,600 |
| 40% | $27.00 | 900 | $24,300 | $8,100 |

**Optimal markdown depth: 20%.** Notice that weekly margin is highest at 0% discount — if we had infinite time, we wouldn't mark it down at all. However, we choose a 20% markdown because we need to liquidate terminal inventory (clear space or exit the season). A 20% discount provides the necessary volume bump (from 25 to 35 units) with the least damage to margin. The basic throw is less elastic — deeper discounts (30-40%) destroy margin without proportionally increasing volume.

**Key insight:** Fashion/decorative products respond better to markdowns (higher elasticity). Basics respond less. This should shape your markdown depth decisions.

## David's Bi-Annual Discount List: The Process

David, Director of Planning, "proposes & finalizes the bi-annual discount list basis on product movement & aging." Here's how this process works:

### Step 1: Aging Analysis (Week 12 of 16-week season)

Pull all products with concerning aging profiles:

| Product | Receipt Date | Age (Days) | Current Stock | ROS (last 4 wks) | WOC | Sell-Through |
|---------|-------------|-----------|--------------|-------------------|-----|-------------|
| Floral Cushion A | Mar 15 | 90 | 340 | 12/wk | 28.3 | 43% |
| Geometric Runner | Mar 1 | 104 | 280 | 5/wk | 56.0 | 22% |
| Linen Curtain Set | Apr 1 | 73 | 180 | 15/wk | 12.0 | 62% |
| Block Print Table Mat | Feb 15 | 118 | 420 | 3/wk | 140.0 | 12% |
| Ceramic Lamp | Mar 20 | 85 | 90 | 8/wk | 11.3 | 55% |

### Step 2: Triage

| Product | WOC | Sell-Through | Verdict | Action |
|---------|-----|-------------|---------|--------|
| Floral Cushion A | 28.3 | 43% | **At risk** — too much stock for remaining season | 20% markdown |
| Geometric Runner | 56.0 | 22% | **Critical** — will not sell at current pace | 30% markdown |
| Linen Curtain Set | 12.0 | 62% | **Healthy** — on track | No action |
| Block Print Table Mat | 140.0 | 12% | **Terminal** — needs aggressive clearance | 40% markdown + reallocation |
| Ceramic Lamp | 11.3 | 55% | **Good** — slightly ahead of plan | No action |

### Step 3: Build the Discount List

| Product | Current MRP | Proposed Discount | Sale Price | Margin at Sale Price | Projected Additional Units |
|---------|------------|-------------------|-----------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Floral Cushion A | $30.00 | 20% | $24.00 | 52.5% | 3,600 |
| Geometric Runner | $22.00 | 30% | $15.40 | 44.8% | 3,000 |
| Block Print Table Mat | $15.00 | 40% | $9.00 | 22.2% | 5,000 |

### Step 4: Impact Projection

**Markdown Cost = Σ (Projected Units at Discount × Discount per Unit)**

| Product | Projected Units | Discount/Unit | Markdown Cost |
|---------|----------------|--------------|--------------|
| Floral Cushion A | 3,600 | $6.00 | $21,600 |
| Geometric Runner | 3,000 | $6.60 | $19,800 |
| Block Print Table Mat | 5,000 | $6.00 | $30,000 |
| **Total** | | | **$71,400** |

This $71,400 is the cost of markdowns. But what's the BENEFIT?

**Markdown Revenue = Σ (Units × Sale Price)**

| Product | Units | Sale Price | Revenue | Margin |
|---------|-------|-----------|---------|--------|
| Floral Cushion A | 3,600 | $24.00 | $86,400 | $45,360 |
| Geometric Runner | 3,000 | $15.40 | $46,200 | $20,700 |
| Block Print Table Mat | 5,000 | $9.00 | $45,000 | $10,000 |
| **Total** | **11,600** | | **$177,600** | **$76,060** |

**The alternative (no markdown):** These 11,600 units sit unsold, aging further. Next season's markdown will be deeper (50-60%). Or they're written off entirely (cost recovery = $0).

The markdown generates $76,060 of gross margin that would otherwise be $0 (write-off) or deeply negative (60% off later).

## The Markdown-or-Reallocate Decision

Before every markdown, ask: can I fix this with reallocation?

### Decision Matrix

| Question | If Yes | If No |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Is the product selling well in SOME stores? | Reallocate from slow to fast stores first | Product-level problem, markdown needed |
| Is the cost of transfer < the markdown saving? | Reallocate (transfer costs $2-3/unit, markdown costs $9-15/unit) | Markdown directly |
| Is there time for reallocation to work (4+ weeks)? | Reallocate | Not enough time, markdown |
| Does the slow store have an alternative customer base? | Maybe wait — different customer might buy in a different season | Markdown |

### Worked Example

Product X has 200 units in 10 stores, selling poorly (2 units/week total). But 3 of those stores sell 1.5 units/week (strong!) while 7 stores sell 0.07 units/week (dead).

**Option A: Blanket Markdown (30% off all 200 units)**
Assume 100% clearance for simplicity.
- Revenue: 200 × $21.00 = $4,200 — COGS: 200 × $11.40 = $2,280 — Margin: $1,920
**Option B: Reallocation (Transfer 140 units from 7 slow stores to 3 fast stores)**
Fast stores now have 200 units total. Assume 70% sell-through at full price, remaining 30% clear at 20% markdown.
- Transfer cost: 140 × $2.50 = $350 — Full price revenue (140 units): 140 × $30.00 = $4,200 — Markdown revenue (60 units at 20% off): 60 × $24.00 = $1,440 — Total Revenue: $5,640
- Total COGS: 200 × $11.40 = $2,280 — Total Margin: $5,640 − $2,280 − $350 = $3,010
**Option C: Targeted Markdown**
Keep full price in 3 fast stores, markdown 30% in 7 slow stores. Assume 100% sell-through for simplicity.
- Fast stores (60 units at $30.00): $1,800 — Slow stores (140 units at $21.00): $2,940 — Total Revenue: $4,740 — Total COGS: 200 × $11.40 = $2,280 — Total Margin: $4,740 − $2,280 = $2,460
**Conclusion:** Option B (Reallocation) yields the highest margin ($3,010) but takes time and logistics effort. Option C (Targeted) preserves more margin than Option A (Blanket) because it avoids subsidizing customers in stores where the product was selling fine at full price.

**Key learning:** Blanket markdowns across all stores destroy margin in stores where the product was selling fine. Use store-level or cluster-level markdown when possible.

## EOSS (End of Season Sale) Strategy

### The EOSS Calendar

Most Indian retailers run two major EOSS events:
- **Summer EOSS:** July-August (clearing Spring/Summer stock)
- **Winter EOSS:** January-February (clearing Autumn/Winter/Festive stock)

### Planning the EOSS

| Planning Element | Detail |
|-----------------|--------|
| **Trigger** | Season officially ends (last 4-6 weeks) |
| **Products included** | All products with sell-through < 70% at trigger date |
| **Discount tiers** | Graduated: 20% → 30% → 50% (deeper as EOSS progresses) |
| **Duration** | 4-6 weeks |
| **Target** | Clear 80% of remaining seasonal stock |
| **Residual** | Remaining 20% → outlet, online clearance, write-off, or carry-over |

### EOSS Margin Impact

| EOSS Week | Discount | % of EOSS Sales | Revenue Quality |
|-----------|---------|----------------|----------------|
| Week 1-2 | 20% off | 35% | Decent — margin still positive |
| Week 3-4 | 30% off | 40% | Moderate — margin tight |
| Week 5-6 | 50% off | 25% | Margin-negative on some products |

## Markdown Metrics: What to Track

### 1. Markdown Rate

**Markdown Rate (%) = (Total Markdown Value ($)) / (Net Sales ($)) × 100**

**Target:** < 8% for Home & Lifestyle (lower for basics, higher for fashion)

### 2. Full-Price Sell-Through

**Full-Price ST (%) = (Units Sold at Full Price) / (Total Units Sold) × 100**

**Target:** > 65% for seasonal products, > 85% for basics

### 3. Markdown Efficiency

**MD Efficiency = (Additional Units Sold Post-Markdown) / (Total Remaining Stock at Markdown Date) × 100**

If you marked down 500 remaining units and sold 350 additional:

**MD Efficiency = (350) / (500) × 100 = 70%**

70% markdown efficiency is good. Below 50% suggests the discount wasn't deep enough or the product has fundamental demand issues.

### 4. Markdown-Adjusted Margin

**MD-Adjusted GM% = ((Full Price Rev × Full Price GM%) + (MD Rev × MD GM%)) / (Total Revenue)**

## Strategic Trade-Offs & Risk Matrices

- **Margin vs. Working Capital:** Every markdown sacrifices margin to unlock cash trapped in slow-moving inventory.
- **Early/Shallow vs. Late/Deep:** Taking a markdown early preserves more margin per unit but cuts the full-price selling window short. Waiting longer gives full-price sales a chance but requires much deeper cuts to clear the remaining stock.
- **Reallocation vs. Markdowns:** Reallocation saves margin but incurs logistics costs and takes time. Markdowns are instant but immediately destroy margin.

---

## Strategic & Operational Pitfalls & Misconceptions

1. **Marking down too late:** Waiting until Week 14 of a 16-week season means you have 2 weeks to clear what didn't sell in 14. You'll need 50%+ off. If you'd marked down at Week 10, 20-25% off might have been sufficient.
2. **Blanket markdowns across all stores:** Products selling well in Store A don't need a markdown just because Store B can't sell them. Use targeted markdowns.
3. **Marking down before diagnosing:** A product with 30% sell-through might just be in the wrong stores. Check store-level data before discounting. Reallocation is cheaper.
4. **Going too deep too fast:** Jumping straight to 50% off leaves no room for a graduated approach. Start at 15-20% and escalate only if velocity doesn't respond.
5. **Not tracking the markdown's effectiveness:** After marking down, did velocity actually improve? If you discounted by 30% and weekly sales went from 10 to 12, the markdown isn't working. Go deeper or try a different approach.
6. **Conditioning customers to wait for markdowns:** If your EOSS is predictable (always starts July 1, always 30% off), savvy customers will stop buying in June and wait. Consider varying timing and depth to reduce predictability.
7. **Not accounting for markdown in the original OTB:** If your OTB assumed 4% markdowns but actual markdowns run at 7%, your margin plan is busted. Build realistic markdown assumptions into the OTB from the start.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Diagnose before you discount.** Wrong store ≠ markdown needed. Reallocation is cheaper.

2. **Time markdowns deliberately.** Early + shallow beats late + deep. Use the 65% sell-through trigger at the two-thirds mark of the season.

3. **Graduate the discount.** 20% → 30% → 50%, not straight to 50%.

4. **Target markdowns by store when possible.** Don't subsidize customers in stores where the product is selling fine.

5. **Track markdown effectiveness.** If velocity doesn't respond to the discount, the problem isn't price — go deeper or try another approach.

6. **The true cost of markdowns is 1.5-2× the visible discount.** Factor in conditioning, brand perception, and operational costs.

7. **Build realistic markdown assumptions into OTB.** Under-planning markdowns guarantees a margin miss.
