# Module 1.3: Open-to-Buy (OTB) — The Financial Spine of Planning

> *"OTB is not a spreadsheet. It's a contract between the planner and the business. It says: 'Given these sales targets and these margin goals, this is exactly how much capital we can allocate to inventory, and when.' Every dollar of buying beyond OTB is an unhedged bet against shareholder return."*

## Executive Summary & Core Dilemma

**How does a retail executive translate revenue forecasts and margin targets into a rigorously controlled capital allocation framework — month by month, dollar by dollar — while optimizing working capital, maintaining liquidity, and maximizing enterprise value?**

Both David, Director of Planning, and Sarah, VP of Merchandising, cite OTB as a core strategic competency. David "architects the OTB and assortment frameworks." Sarah built "analytics-led capital allocation models." OTB is the fulcrum of merchandise planning. Without it, procurement lacks financial discipline, working capital is squandered on excess inventory, and P&L outcomes become erratic, jeopardizing stakeholder trust.

---

## What OTB Is (First Principles)

### The Simplest Definition

**Open-to-Buy is the quantum of capital authorized for procurement of new merchandise within a specific fiscal period.**

Consider it analogous to enterprise capital allocation:
- The enterprise forecasts $100,000/month in revenue (sales plan)
- The enterprise holds $40,000 in existing asset value (beginning inventory)
- The enterprise requires $35,000 in terminal asset value at period end (ending inventory target)
- The enterprise projects $60,000 in cost of goods sold
- The allowable capital deployment for new assets? That is the OTB.

### The Fundamental OTB Equation

**OTB = Planned Sales + Planned Markdowns + Planned EOM Stock - BOM Stock - On Order**

Let's unpack each component:

| Component | Strategic Meaning | Data Source |
|-----------|------------------|-------------|
| **Planned Sales** | Projected top-line revenue at retail valuation | Demand forecasting models (Module 1.1) |
| **Planned Markdowns** | Projected value erosion required to maintain inventory velocity | Historical degradation rates + promotional strategy |
| **Planned EOM Stock** | Target terminal inventory to secure forward revenue | Forward month's Stock-to-Sales Ratio (SSR) |
| **BOM Stock** | Capital tied up in existing inventory at period start | Prior period's EOM actuals |
| **On Order** | Capital already committed to the supply chain | Open purchase orders in the pipeline |

### The OTB Equation — In Executive Terms

> "To secure our top-line targets and facilitate necessary inventory liquidation, while achieving our terminal stock objectives, we must deploy a specific amount of capital. Factoring in our current asset base and forward commitments, the residual represents our authorized capital deployment—our Open-to-Buy."

## Why OTB Exists (The Problem It Solves)

### Without OTB

| Operational Event | Enterprise Consequence |
|------------------|------------------------|
| Merchandising identifies a high-potential assortment expansion → commits $5 Million | Unregulated capital deployment occurs outside fiscal guardrails |
| Category is already experiencing inventory bloat | $5 Million exacerbates working capital inefficiency |
| Liquidity is heavily constrained | CFO and Treasury flag working capital distress |
| End of fiscal period: 40% of inventory remains unmonetized | Catastrophic margin erosion via markdowns; EPS miss |
| Post-mortem: "Why did we over-leverage?" | Lack of systemic capital controls |

### With OTB

| Operational Event | Enterprise Consequence |
|------------------|------------------------|
| Merchandising identifies a high-potential assortment expansion → requests $5 Million | Planning validates: "OTB framework allows $3.5 Million deployment this period" |
| Strategic Decision: Deploy $3.5 Million now, phase $1.5 Million into subsequent period | Procurement operates within strict governance guardrails |
| End of fiscal period: Inventory aligns with strategic targets | Margin preserved; robust free cash flow generation |
| Post-mortem: "Capital was deployed efficiently" | Sustained enterprise value creation |

**OTB is the financial governance mechanism that prevents speculative or undisciplined procurement from eroding enterprise value.**

## Building an OTB: Step by Step

### Step 1: Set the Planning Period

OTB is typically structured around a **6-month fiscal season** (Spring/Summer or Autumn/Winter/Festive), segmented into **monthly tranches**.

For a Spring/Summer season at Aura Global Retail's Home & Lifestyle category:
- Fiscal period: April to September (6 months)
- Monthly OTB tranches synchronized with the corporate calendar

### Step 2: Plan Sales (The Revenue Line)

This is derived from robust demand forecasting (Module 1.1), recalibrated for seasonality and macroeconomic indicators.

**Example — SS Season Revenue Plan:**

| Month | Sales Plan ($ Million at Retail) |
|-------|----------------------------------|
| April | 10.3 |
| May | 9.5 |
| June | 8.4 |
| July | 7.6 |
| August | 9.0 |
| September | 10.9 |
| **Season Total** | **55.7** |

### Step 3: Plan Markdowns

Markdowns are budgeted as a percentage of revenue, informed by historical elasticity, lifecycle modeling, and promotional strategy.

**Typical markdown architecture:**

| Markdown Component | % of Sales | Strategic Rationale |
|-------------------|-----------|---------------------|
| In-season tactical promotions | 2.0% | Drive traffic and event-based volume |
| End-of-season liquidation | 4.0% | Clear obsolete working capital |
| Staff/corporate allowances | 0.5% | Organizational policy |
| **Total Value Erosion Budget** | **6.5%** | |

For a $55.7 Million season: Total markdown budget = $3.62 Million

**Monthly markdown allocation:**

| Month | Markdown Plan ($ Million) |
|-------|---------------------------|
| April | 0.20 |
| May | 0.20 |
| June | 0.55 (Mid-season promotional event) |
| July | 0.80 (Liquidation phase initiates) |
| August | 1.00 (Liquidation phase peaks) |
| September | 0.87 (Residual clearance) |
| **Total** | **3.62** |

### Step 4: Plan Stock Levels (BOM and EOM)

This phase utilizes the **Stock-to-Sales Ratio (SSR)**. SSR defines the capital intensity required to support projected revenue generation.

**BOM Stock = Planned Monthly Sales × Target SSR**

**Optimizing SSR across the fiscal timeline:**

SSR is dynamic. Higher capital intensity is required ahead of demand peaks; lower intensity suffices during troughs. Aura Global Retail's Home & Lifestyle SSR typically oscillates between 2.5 and 4.0.

| Month | Sales Plan | Target SSR | BOM Stock Required ($ Million) |
|-------|-----------|------------|--------------------------------|
| April | 10.3 | 3.5 | 36.05 |
| May | 9.5 | 3.5 | 33.25 |
| June | 8.4 | 3.0 | 25.20 |
| July | 7.6 | 3.0 | 22.80 |
| August | 9.0 | 3.0 | 27.00 |
| September | 10.9 | 3.5 | 38.15 |
| **EOM September** | — | 2.5 | **27.25** (Target terminal stock) |

**Note:** The mathematical continuity requires that a period's ending inventory equals the subsequent period's beginning inventory (e.g., April EOM = May BOM = $33.25 Million).

### Step 5: Calculate OTB

Applying the fundamental equation sequentially:

**OTB = Planned Sales + Planned Markdowns + Planned EOM Stock - BOM Stock**

(Assuming zero initial pipeline commitments for theoretical clarity)

| Month | Sales | Markdowns | EOM Stock | BOM Stock | **OTB ($ Million)** |
|-------|-------|-----------|-----------|-----------|--------------------|
| April | 10.3 | 0.20 | 33.25 | 36.05 | **7.70** |
| May | 9.5 | 0.20 | 25.20 | 33.25 | **1.65** |
| June | 8.4 | 0.55 | 22.80 | 25.20 | **6.55** |
| July | 7.6 | 0.80 | 27.00 | 22.80 | **12.60** |
| August | 9.0 | 1.00 | 38.15 | 27.00 | **21.15** |
| September | 10.9 | 0.87 | 27.25 | 38.15 | **0.87** |
| **Total** | **55.7** | **3.62** | | | **50.52** |

### Reading the OTB

- **April OTB = $7.7 Million:** You are authorized to inject $7.7 Million of fresh inventory. Opening the season with robust stock levels ($36.05 Million BOM) means capital deployment is structurally moderate.
- **May OTB = $1.65 Million:** A severely constrained allocation. Elevated BOM relative to softer May revenue necessitates capital preservation. If pipeline orders exist for May delivery, you are likely OVER-BOUGHT, risking liquidity.
- **July OTB = $12.6 Million:** Strategic inventory accumulation ahead of the August-September demand surge.
- **August OTB = $21.15 Million:** Peak capital deployment, positioning the enterprise for maximum revenue capture during the festive apex.
- **September OTB = $0.87 Million:** End-of-cycle capital restriction. Procurement ceases as the focus shifts to liquidating inventory toward the $27.25 Million terminal target.

### Step 6: Convert to Cost

OTB at retail expresses the top-line valuation of incoming assets. To determine the actual outflow of working capital to suppliers, we translate to cost:

**OTB at Cost = OTB at Retail × (1 - IMU%)**

Assuming an Initial Markup (IMU) of 62%:

**OTB at Cost = $50.52 Million × (1 - 0.62) = $50.52 Million × 0.38 = $19.20 Million**

This $19.20 Million represents the actual cash obligation the enterprise is committing to its vendor base.

## The Complete OTB Format: The WSSI

At the operational nexus, OTB translates into the **WSSI — Weekly Sales, Stock, and Intake** ledger. The WSSI is the granular, dynamic control panel for managing working capital velocity.

### WSSI Structure (Weekly Cadence)

| Week | BOS | Receipts | Sales Plan | Markdowns | Shrinkage | EOS | WOC |
|------|-----|----------|------------|-----------|-----------|-----|-----|
| W1 | 36.05 | 2.00 | 2.50 | 0.05 | 0.02 | 35.48 | 14.2 |
| W2 | 35.48 | 1.50 | 2.60 | 0.05 | 0.02 | 34.31 | 13.2 |
| W3 | 34.31 | 1.80 | 2.55 | 0.05 | 0.02 | 33.49 | 13.1 |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

*(BOS = Beginning of Stock, EOS = End of Stock, all financial values in $ Million)*

**The WSSI Equation:**

**EOS = BOS + Receipts - Sales - Markdowns - Shrinkage**

This is the OTB formula transposed to dynamically solve for terminal working capital.

**The strategic imperative of a weekly cadence:** Monthly OTB dictates macroeconomic strategy; weekly WSSI dictates tactical execution. If Week 2 sales underperform by 15%, the resulting bloat in Week 3's BOS is immediately visible, triggering executive interventions to defer pipeline receipts and protect liquidity.

## In-Season OTB Management

### The OTB Variance Report

Periodic reconciliation of actual execution against the strategic baseline is non-negotiable.

| Line Item | Strategic Plan | Actual Performance | Variance | Enterprise Impact |
|-----------|----------------|--------------------|----------|-------------------|
| Sales | $10.3 Million | $9.5 Million | -$0.8 Million (-7.8%) | Revenue shortfall; asset stagnation |
| Markdowns | $0.2 Million | $0.35 Million | +$0.15 Million (+75.0%) | Accelerated margin compression |
| Receipts | $7.7 Million | $8.2 Million | +$0.5 Million (+6.5%) | Unauthorized capital utilization |
| EOM Stock | $33.25 Million | $34.8 Million | +$1.55 Million (+4.7%) | Bloated working capital carried into May |

**Executive Diagnosis:** Top-line execution failed, margin was sacrificed attempting to stimulate demand, AND procurement exceeded authorized levels. The compound result: ending inventory is $1.55 Million over budget. This represents a critical failure in capital efficiency that demands immediate structural correction.

### OTB Reforecasting Triggers

Strategic recalibration (reforecasting) is mandated when variances breach acceptable control limits.

| Variance Trigger | Control Limit Threshold | Required Executive Action |
|------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Revenue Shortfall | 2 consecutive weeks > 10% below plan | Reforecast revenue downward; aggressively curtail forward pipeline |
| Revenue Outperformance | 2 consecutive weeks > 10% above plan | Reforecast revenue upward; deploy liquidity reserve to capture upside |
| Margin Degradation | Actual markdowns > 150% of plan | Restructure margin expectations; restrict further procurement |
| Supply Chain Disruption | Critical pipeline delayed > 2 weeks | Re-architect the intake phasing to stabilize assortment |
| Working Capital Bloat | EOM stock > 110% of planned target | Freeze or heavily slash subsequent period's authorized OTB |

**The Reforecasting Protocol:**

1. **Root-Cause Analysis:** Diagnose the variance. Is it a macroeconomic shift, a localized merchandising failure, or a supply chain constraint?
2. **Forward Recalibration:** Adjust forward projections for revenue, value erosion, and capital deployment.
3. **Restate the OTB:** Generate a revised capital authorization matrix for the remainder of the fiscal period.
4. **Stakeholder Alignment:** Syndicate the revised plan across Merchandising, Supply Chain, and Corporate Finance.

## The OTB-to-Margin Bridge

OTB is ostensibly an inventory framework, but it is fundamentally a margin engineering tool.

### Constructing the Margin Bridge

| Financial Element | Value ($ Million) | Profitability Impact |
|-------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Planned Sales (at retail) | 55.70 | Top-line Revenue |
| Planned Cost of Goods (at IMU 62%) | 21.17 | Direct Asset Cost |
| **Planned IMU Margin** | **34.53 (62.0%)** | Gross Margin Baseline |
| − Planned Markdowns | 3.62 | −6.5% Yield Erosion |
| − Planned Shrinkage (1%) | 0.56 | −1.0% Loss Provision |
| **Planned Maintained Margin** | **30.35 (54.5%)** | Target Enterprise Gross Margin |

**The Imperative of Cross-Functional Alignment:** Merchandising often views OTB as a "budget for product innovation." Planning and Finance view OTB as "a capital structure designed to yield a 54.5% maintained margin." 

If Merchandising advocates for aggressive procurement (inflating receipts), the margin architecture shifts:
- Increased asset density → elevated risk of obsolescence → mandatory clearance → compromised maintained margin.
- The Planning executive's mandate is to quantify this risk: "Authorizing a $5 Million procurement overage will inflate projected markdowns by $X, depressing maintained margin from 54.5% to a structurally unacceptable 52.8%."

## OTB Liquidity Reserve: The Chase Budget

Astute capital allocators never deploy 100% of their authorization preemptively. Establishing a liquidity reserve (colloquially, a "chase budget") is essential for risk mitigation and opportunistic growth.

**Strategic Benchmark:** Retain 10-20% of the total OTB as uncommitted liquidity.

**Applying the Framework:** Total Authorized OTB = $50.52 Million. Liquidity Reserve (15%) = $7.58 Million. Firm Pre-Season Commitment = $42.94 Million.

**Strategic Deployment of Liquidity:**
- **Accelerating Winners:** A product line is vastly outperforming; inject capital to maximize yield.
- **Market Arbitrage:** A supplier offers distressed premium assets at a steep discount; deploy capital to secure outsized margin.
- **Competitive Exploitation:** A competitor falters; deploy inventory to aggressively capture market share.

**When to Withhold Capital:**
- "Because the budget exists." Uncommitted capital is a strategic buffer, not an entitlement.
- Subsidizing failure. Injecting capital into underperforming assortments is value-destructive; recognize the sunk cost and preserve liquidity.

## OTB at Different Planning Levels

### Top-Down OTB (Sarah's Level)

Initiates from corporate financial mandates and cascades downwards:

```
Enterprise Target: $1 Billion annual revenue, 60% GM, 3.5x inventory turn
    ↓
Seasonal Tranche: SS = $450 Million, AW/Festive = $550 Million
    ↓
Monthly Phasing: Apply econometric seasonality indices
    ↓
Category Allocation: Textiles 35%, Furniture 30%, Décor 25%, Accessories 10%
```

### Bottom-Up OTB (David's Level)

Builds organically from granular product-level modeling:

```
SKU/Category Modeling: Cushions $X, Curtains $Y, Lamps $Z...
    ↓
Aggregate to Division: Total Built = $480 Million
    ↓
Reconcile with Corporate Mandate: Does the micro align with the macro?
    ↓
Resolution: If bottom-up demands $480 Million but Top-Down authorizes $450 Million, a structural negotiation is required.
```

### Reconciliation

Top-down targets and bottom-up models rarely achieve immediate parity.

| Scenario | Strategic Implication | Resolution Protocol |
|----------|-----------------------|---------------------|
| Bottom-Up > Top-Down | Operating units identify growth that Finance hasn't funded | Elevate funding if ROI justifies; otherwise, enforce capital rationing |
| Bottom-Up < Top-Down | Operating units cannot locate sufficient yield to meet corporate targets | Lower top-line expectations or mandate M&A/category expansion |
| Variance < 5% | Strong cross-functional alignment | Proceed with localized tactical adjustments |

**This reconciliation process is the crucible of corporate strategy.** It is where financial ambition is tempered by operational reality.

## How OTB Connects to Your Work

### The OTB-to-Allocation Nexus

Inventory allocation is strictly bounded by OTB parameters. If the framework authorizes $7.7 Million in April receipts, allocation algorithms must distribute from that exact liquidity pool. 

If the OTB is structurally misaligned (e.g., authorizing excessive capital), downstream allocation will inevitably force inventory bloat onto the retail footprint. If it is under-funded, localized stockouts are mathematically guaranteed.

**The Analyst's Mandate:** When regional hubs exhibit chronic over/under-stocking, sophisticated analysts bypass allocation logic and investigate upstream OTB misalignments as the systemic root cause.

### The OTB-to-Dashboard Nexus

Executive telemetry (MIS dashboards) must continuously monitor:
- **Authorized vs. Actual Procurement:** Is capital deployment disciplined?
- **Projected vs. Actual Yield (Sales):** Is the asset base generating the expected return?
- **Target vs. Actual EOM Inventory:** Are working capital levels expanding or contracting versus plan?
- **Liquidity Remaining:** What is our remaining capacity for opportunistic procurement?
- **Weeks of Cover (WOC):** Based on current velocity, when does our asset base deplete?

These are the vital signs David tracks with rigorous frequency.

## Strategic Trade-Offs & Risk Matrices

### Trade-Off 1: Stringent Control vs. Operational Agility
- **Stringent OTB:** Imposes draconian capital discipline and minimizes obsolescence risk, but stifles the ability to exploit emerging market trends.
- **Flexible OTB:** Enhances market responsiveness but escalates the probability of working capital bloat.
- **Resolution:** Institutionalize the liquidity reserve (10-20% uncommitted) to balance fiscal conservatism with entrepreneurial agility.

### Trade-Off 2: Asset Density vs. Asset Freshness
- **High BOM Capitalization:** Maximizes in-stock probability and revenue capture, but degrades inventory age and turn metrics.
- **Low BOM Capitalization:** Ensures a fresh, dynamic assortment and high turnover, but elevates the risk of out-of-stocks and lost revenue.
- **Resolution:** This is the precise optimization problem Sarah solved. The objective is to engineer the minimum viable inventory footprint that sustains targeted service levels.

### Trade-Off 3: Supply Chain Certainty vs. Market Responsiveness
- **Heavy Pre-Season Commitment:** Locks in favorable unit economics and guarantees supply chain capacity, but eliminates in-season agility.
- **Light Pre-Season Commitment:** Preserves maximum optionality, but risks supply chain bottlenecks and margin compression from expedited freight.
- **Resolution:** Secure core/annuity inventory early (60-70% commitment), while reserving capital for fashion/trend items to be sourced rapidly in-season (30-40% chase).

## Strategic & Operational Pitfalls

1. **Viewing OTB merely as a spending allowance.**
OTB is a complex macroeconomic system. It interlocks revenue, inventory valuation, markdowns, and capital deployment. Altering one node shifts the entire equilibrium. 

2. **Equating a high monthly OTB with a mandate to spend.**
A high allocation in a specific month (e.g., August OTB = $21.15 Million) often signifies strategic accumulation ahead of a peak demand cycle. It is not an invitation to initiate new procurement; it is the planned ingestion of existing pipeline commitments.

3. **Failing to penalize the OTB for revenue shortfalls.**
If April revenue underperforms by 8%, and May's OTB is not recalibrated, the enterprise carries bloated working capital into the new period. This excess compounds exponentially, destroying seasonal ROI.

4. **Omitting pipeline commitments from liquidity calculations.**
'On-order' inventory (capital legally committed but not yet physically received) instantly depletes available OTB. If $3.0 Million is committed for May, and the May OTB authorization is $1.65 Million, the adjusted liquidity is deeply negative (-$1.35 Million). The enterprise is in breach of its capital constraints.

5. **Under-provisioning for markdown activity.**
Budgeting 4% for markdowns when historical cohort analysis dictates 8% artificially inflates the OTB. When the inevitable markdowns occur, inventory is depleted faster than modeled, leading to severe downstream stockouts.

6. **Applying a static SSR across cyclical demand curves.**
Peak months require a materially LOWER SSR than off-peak months due to accelerated inventory velocity. Utilizing a static, annualized SSR guarantees working capital inefficiency—bloating off-peak periods and starving peak periods of necessary stock.

7. **Deploying 100% of capital preemptively.**
Fully exhausting the OTB before the season begins obliterates operational agility. The liquidity reserve is not idle cash; it is the strategic optionality required to navigate dynamic market environments.

## Key Takeaways

1. **OTB is a macroeconomic system, not a static budget.** It synchronizes revenue, inventory valuation, markdowns, and procurement into a singular financial architecture. Modifying any variable demands system-wide recalibration.
2. **The Fundamental OTB Equation is the cornerstone of retail finance.** **OTB = Sales + Markdowns + EOM Stock − BOM Stock − On Order**. Master it implicitly.
3. **Dynamic in-season governance is non-negotiable.** Static pre-season planning is insufficient; weekly variance analysis and rigorous reforecasting are required to navigate market volatility.
4. **The liquidity reserve provides strategic optionality.** Withholding 10-20% of capital facilitates opportunistic growth and insulates the balance sheet from unanticipated shocks.
5. **Reconciling Top-Down mandates with Bottom-Up realities is where true strategy is forged.** The tension between financial rigor and operational optimism produces robust business plans.
6. **Capital over-deployment (over-buying) is insidious and compounding.** An over-bought position calcifies working capital, necessitating margin-destroying markdowns that cripple seasonal profitability.
7. **Downstream execution (allocation) is entirely dependent on upstream OTB discipline.** Understanding the financial constraints of the OTB illuminates the rationale behind inventory positioning across the retail network.
