## What Are Vendor Economics? (First Principles)

Vendor Economics is the study of how your supplier generates a profit from the orders you place. 

In retail, the relationship is often viewed antagonistically: The retailer wants the lowest possible cost, and the vendor wants the highest possible price. However, a sustainable supply chain requires a symbiotic relationship. If you squeeze a vendor so hard that they lose money on your order, they will eventually go bankrupt, cut corners on quality, or drop you as a client. 

### The Vendor's P&L

Just as you have a Category P&L (Module 2.4), your vendor has a Factory P&L. 

**Vendor's Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) includes:**
1. **Raw Materials:** Fabric, clay, wood, dyes. (Often 50-60% of total cost).
2. **Direct Labor:** Artisan wages, machine operator wages.
3. **Factory Overhead:** Electricity, machine maintenance, factory rent.

**Vendor's Operating Expenses (OpEx) includes:**
1. **Administrative Costs:** Management salaries, office expenses.
2. **Financing Costs:** The interest they pay on the loans they take out to buy the raw materials for *your* order. (Crucial).

If a vendor sells a hand-carved wooden tray to Aura Global Retail for $25:
- Raw Wood: $10
- Labor to carve: $7.50
- Factory Overhead: $2.50
- **Total COGS: $20**
- **Vendor Gross Profit: $5(20%)**
- Minus OpEx & Interest: $3
- **Vendor Net Profit: $2(8%)**

## The Capital Cycle Constraint

This is the most critical concept in vendor management, especially in India where credit is expensive. 

When you place a Purchase Order (PO) for $100,000, you do not pay the vendor immediately. Aura Global Retail might have payment terms of "Net 60" (paying 60 days after the goods are delivered). 

However, the vendor has to buy raw materials *today*. They have to pay their labor *every week*. 

**The Timeline of Cash for a Vendor:**
- Day 0: PO received from Aura Global Retail.
- Day 5: Vendor buys raw materials (Cash Outflow: -$50,000).
- Day 15-45: Production happens. Wages paid weekly (Cash Outflow: -$30,000).
- Day 60: Goods shipped to the Aura Global Retail DC. (Zero Cash Inflow).
- Day 120 (60 days after delivery): Aura Global Retail pays the invoice (Cash Inflow: +$100,000).

For 115 days, the vendor is operating in a massive cash deficit. How do they survive? They take out working capital loans from a bank at 12% to 18% annual interest. 

### Why Planners Must Care About the Capital Cycle

If you suddenly double your order size (because a product is selling well), a naive planner expects the vendor to be thrilled. "More business!" 
But if the vendor is a small artisan group, they might not have the credit line to borrow the cash needed to buy twice as much raw material. 
**Result:** They accept the order, fail to buy enough raw materials in time, delay production, and miss the delivery date by 4 weeks. You experience a massive stockout. 

**The Lesson:** Vendor capacity is not just about the number of sewing machines. It is equally about their line of credit.

## Minimum Order Quantities (MOQs)

MOQs are the bane of every planner's existence, but they exist for mathematical reasons, not out of vendor stubbornness. 

### Why MOQs Exist
1. **Raw Material Yields:** A fabric mill requires a minimum of 1,000 meters to run a dyeing vat efficiently. If 1,000 meters yields 500 bedsheets, the vendor's MOQ to you is 500 bedsheets. If you only want 200, the vendor has to absorb the cost of 300 unsold bedsheets, wiping out their profit.
2. **Setup and Changeover Costs:** Setting up a machine (or a block-printing table) takes hours of labor. 
   - Setup Cost = $5,000.   - If order is 100 units: Setup cost per unit = $50.   - If order is 1,000 units: Setup cost per unit = $5.   - Small orders destroy the vendor's margins through setup costs.

### Negotiating Around MOQs
When David, Director of Planning, is planning ISQ (Module 1.4), he often finds the ideal buy quantity is below the vendor's MOQ.

**Strategy 1: Consolidate Colors (Width to Depth)**
Instead of buying 200 units each of Red, Blue, and Green (violating a 500-unit MOQ per color), buy 600 units of Blue. 

**Strategy 2: The Raw Material Commitment (Greige Fabric)**
Commit to buying the 1,000 meters of raw (greige) fabric upfront. Tell the vendor: "Dye 300 meters now and hold the rest. We will pay for the fabric now." This removes the vendor's risk and allows smaller, more agile production runs.

## Production Capacity and Seasonality

A factory has a maximum number of units it can produce in a month. 
Let's say a ceramic vendor can produce 10,000 mugs a month. 

In retail, demand is highly seasonal (Diwali, Christmas). 
If Aura Global Retail needs 5,000 mugs a month from Jan-Aug, the vendor is operating at 50% capacity. 
If Aura Global Retail suddenly needs 25,000 mugs in September for Diwali, the vendor physically cannot produce them in one month. 

### The Planner's Solution: Level Loading
Planners must smooth out production for the vendor. 
Instead of ordering 25,000 mugs in September, the planner orders 10,000 in July, 10,000 in August, and 5,000 in September. Aura Global Retail holds the inventory in the DC for a few extra months (incurring carrying costs) to ensure the vendor can actually fulfill the demand. 

**The Trade-Off:** 
Carrying cost of holding inventory early vs. The cost of empty shelves during the peak season because the vendor choked on a massive last-minute order.

## The True Cost of "Agility"

Retailers love the word "Agile." They want to place small orders, read the sales data, and chase the winners with fast replenishment. 

**The Vendor's View of Agility:**
Agility is a nightmare for factory efficiency. It means small batch sizes, frequent machine changeovers, and unpredictable raw material needs. 

If you want a vendor to be agile (e.g., delivering replenishment in 3 weeks instead of 12 weeks), you must pay for that agility. 
- You pay higher unit costs (to cover their inefficiency).
- You commit to raw material liability.
- You reserve and pay for their factory capacity even if you don't use it.

Agility is not free.

## Strategic Trade-Offs & Risk Matrices

### Trade-Off 1: Vendor Consolidation vs. Risk Diversification
- **Consolidation:** Giving all your ceramic business to one massive factory. You get incredible volume discounts (lower COGS) and they prioritize your orders. **Risk:** If that factory burns down, or faces a labor strike, your entire category goes to zero.
- **Diversification:** Splitting the business across three smaller factories. You pay slightly higher prices due to lower volume per factory. **Risk Reduction:** If one fails, the others can ramp up.

### Trade-Off 2: Low Unit Cost vs. Payment Terms
A vendor offers you a cushion cover for $10 if you pay cash upfront (Day 0), or $11 if you want Net 90 payment terms. 
Which is better? It depends on your company's Cost of Capital (WACC). For most retailers, conserving cash is critical, so they accept the slightly higher unit cost in exchange for keeping cash in the bank for 90 days.

## Strategic & Operational Pitfalls

1. **Believing "Bigger Order = Faster Production":** Planners assume a $1 Million order will get priority and be produced faster than a $100,000 order. Reality: The $1 Million order takes 10x more raw materials and labor, often resulting in *longer* lead times unless planned months in advance.
2. **Ignoring the Vendor's Sub-Tier Supply Chain:** You blame the sewing factory for being late, but they are late because the thread supplier was late. A good planner understands the raw material dependencies.
3. **The "Cancel Order" Weapon:** Planners cancel POs when sales slow down. This leaves the vendor holding raw materials they bought for you. Do this enough, and the vendor builds a "risk premium" into their prices for you next year. You pay for cancellations eventually.

## Case Application & Discussion Questions

### Exercise 1: The Cash Flow Gap (30 minutes)
A vendor receives a PO for 10,000 block-print quilts at $50 cost each (Total $500,000). 
- Fabric cost ($250,000) is paid on Day 10.
- Labor cost ($150,000) is paid evenly over 30 days of production (Day 15 to Day 45).
- Goods delivered Day 60. 
- Aura Global Retail pays on Net 60 terms (Day 120).
If the vendor's bank charges 1.5% interest per month on borrowed working capital, calculate the approximate interest cost the vendor absorbs just to fulfill this order. 

### Exercise 2: MOQ Math (20 minutes)
You need to buy Printed Table Runners. 
Vendor A has an MOQ of 1,000 units at $10 each.
Vendor B has an MOQ of 300 units at $13 each. 
Your OTB only allows for a buy of 400 units. 
Option 1: Use Vendor B (buy 400 units × $13).
Option 2: Use Vendor A (buy 1,000 units × $10). 
Calculate the total capital outlay for both. Calculate the trapped working capital if you only expect to sell 400 units this season. Which option is financially sound?

### Exercise 3: Level Loading Production (30 minutes)
You expect demand for a specific Diya to be:
July: 2,000 | Aug: 2,000 | Sept: 15,000 | Oct: 25,000
The vendor's maximum capacity is 12,000 units per month. 
Design a level-loaded PO schedule (how many units they should produce each month from July to October) to meet demand without exceeding their monthly capacity. Assume you can hold inventory in your DC.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Vendors are partners, not vending machines.** If they don't make a sustainable profit, your supply chain will eventually collapse.
2. **The Capital Cycle dictates capacity.** A vendor's ability to take a large order is constrained by their ability to finance the raw materials while waiting 90+ days for your payment.
3. **MOQs are driven by raw material yields and setup costs.** They can be negotiated by committing to raw material liabilities (greige fabric).
4. **Agility has a hard cost.** Fast turnarounds require factory inefficiencies, which you will pay for in unit cost or capacity reservations.
5. **Level Loading protects peak season.** You must use your DC to hold inventory produced during slow months to ensure you have enough stock during peak months when vendor capacity maxes out.
